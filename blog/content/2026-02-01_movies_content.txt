<p>While doing some research for an article recently, I was looking through the Chateaugay Record issues from the 1920s and 30s. I kept coming across ads and news items about movies playing in theater spaces from Malone to Ellenburg. </p>

<p>In the ‘20s, the silent film era was in full swing. Silents were being shown in Churubusco, Ellenburg Depot, Ellenburg Center, Brainardsville (in the Grange building), Burke, and Chateaugay. </p>

<p>With the dawning of the “Talkies” in 1927 and the release of “The Jazz Singer” starring Al Jolson, the movie craze became an even more prominent part of the area’s entertainment culture. The “Golden Age of Hollywood” had begun. </p>

<p>The Golden Age generally refers to the era from the late 1920s to the early 1960s, kicked off by “The Jazz Singer”. It was dominated by just five major studios: MGM, Fox, RKO (Radio-Keith-Orpheum formed by the RCA Corp.), Paramount, and Warner Bros. The five studios controlled everything: the stars, production, distribution, marketing, etc.</p>

<p>The introduction of sound and the development of what was marketed as Technicolor, were the major drivers of this motion picture popularity. This led to the development of different film genres like westerns, comedies, musicals, and mystery and suspense thrillers. The idea of the “blockbuster” movie was emerging rapidly. </p>

<p>Anti-trust legislation in the 1950s would lead to the breakup of the Studio System, ending the strangle hold the five major studios had on the industry, and the eventual end of this “Golden Age”.
During the eras of the Silent Films and the Golden Age, movie theaters were everywhere. Even in small towns, movies often ran 7 days a week. </p>

<p>By 1930, here we were fully in the “Golden Age”. Although our immediate area was small, we had many theaters operating, they offered non-stop movies, popcorn and treats, and a wide variety of genres―the formula for a movie fan’s heaven…</p>

<p>We’ve come a long way from the Era of Silent movies and the Golden Age of Hollywood. Since then, movies have been made available on VHS and Beta tapes, on DVDs, and now are readily accessible online through a whole host of streaming services and, of course, can still be seen in theaters. One wonders what the big studio stars from the 20s, 30s, and 40s would think of all this…</p>
