<p>Scott Durant has recently been posting some Chateaugay scenes from his amazing collection of photos of the North Country’s past. These have started some discussion threads about once prominent features within the Village Chateaugay that no longer exist. </p>

<p>I’ve selected three to highlight here: The “Old School” (if you know, you know) that used to sit at the end of Church Street, the Chateaugay Fairgrounds that once sat at the end of Collins Street, and the railroad that ran through the village.</p>

<p>The first image is a Google Earth view to which I’ve added the three locations, so it’s easier to picture them within the modern context. Following that are two views of the school-the first the original Chateaugay Academy and the second is the building just before it was demolished in 1958. Then we have views of the horse racetrack and the grandstand from the fairgrounds. These are followed by views of the railroad passenger depot, steam and diesel engines and the railyard.</p>

<p>The “Old School” stood on Church Street from 1879 until 1958, when it became too small for the growing student population and too dated to operate efficiently and too old to be “modernized”. It was replaced by the new Chateaugay Central School on River Street which has also been added to and improved since the 1950s.</p>

<p>The need for a local agricultural fairground had been talked about and proposed since the 1860s and 1870s, but nothing had gone beyond the discussion stage. Finally, in 1908, the fairgrounds became a reality, and the first annual fair was held that year. Unfortunately, the local Agricultural Society went bankrupt in 1912. The 1911 annual fair became both the 4th fair held on the grounds and the last one to ever use the grounds. The property today is the site of the milk/cheese plant.</p>

<p>The railroad opened in 1850 as the “Northern Railroad” across Northern New York. The New York stations stretched from Rouses Point to Ogdensburg. It was renamed the “Ogdensburg and Lake Champlain Railroad” (O&LC RR) and finally, the Rutland Railroad which stretched from Ogdensburg to Rutland Vermont. In the 1880s, vast tonnage of Chateaugay freight shipments made our local depot the busiest on the entire RR line.</p>

<p>With the development of the new interstate highway system and labor issues on the line, rail passenger service was discontinued, followed by the freight shipments. The line had become too expensive to operate and impossible to be made profitable again. By the mid-1960s, the RR was no more. The tracks were taken up and the railbed sold or deeded to adjacent landowners, etc. 
I remember as a kid, the sound of the train whistle as it would approach Chateaugay. We rode our bikes up to Bova’s grocery (Wendy’s and now Chat Away) and would sit on them watching the train come through, while eating Sealtest ice cream sandwiches in that familiar waxed yellow, brown and red wrapper that we bought from Doug or Pauline for 10 cents.</p>

<p>I went to the Old School as a Kindergartener. My class was the last to begin school there. We lived about half way down Church Street then, and I remember feeling so grown up as I left home and walked up the street to school all by myself. No streets to cross, just walk straight up the street to the end, and there stood the school. I also remember going into the empty old school as a ten-year-old, just before it was torn down. We weren’t supposed to be there and if our parents had found out, we would have been skinned alive. But, in we went, up the fire escape on the north end, shimmying in through the awning window that was ajar enough for us to squeeze through. I can still smell the dank staleness and feel and hear the complete silence inside as we moved away from that window, deeper into the halls and rooms – imagining with each step that anything could befall us, and no one would ever see us again. Our footsteps and hushed voices sounded so very loud as we made our way into the empty, quiet, and decidedly creepy building. I guess, our 10-year-old imaginations made us scared and cautious, but not enough to keep out!</p>

<p>I don’t remember the fairgrounds with the grandstand. But every time we went up to “the Boardman” to fish or play or slide down the hill in the winter, we walked past the old school, crossed the tracks after walking past the McArdle house and the easternmost cheese plant warehouse building, and followed the remains of the fairground racetrack around to the fence at the bottom of Boardman hill. The only other feature of the original fairground I remember was the baseball diamond, complete with seats and bleachers. The field sat within the infield of the original racetrack. I watched many, many Chateaugay Ponies games as I grew up.
Much of Chateaugay’s past, like these three features, has disappeared. The Historical Society strives to keep Chateaugay’s history alive and to tell stories of its fascinating and interesting sights from “back in the day”.>/p>
