<p>Portrait of Rear Admiral Theodorus Bailey now on long-term loan to the Chateaugay Historical Society by the Chateaugay American Legion Post #875</p>

<p>During this past summer, the Chateaugay American Legion Post #875 graciously loaned an oil portrait of Admiral Theodorus Bailey (1805-1877) to the Chateaugay Historical Society for our summer exhibit which involved the very early history of our community.</p>

<p>Admiral Bailey was born in Chateaugay in 1805.  He was the son of Judge William Bailey, one of the earliest settlers in our community. The Admiral joined The United States Navy at 12 years of age and served for the next 47 years, rising to the rank of Rear Admiral.</p>

<p>In 1887, the first veterans’ organization ever formed in Chateaugay was established. It was a post of the Grand Army of the Republic (GAR) and was made up of Civil War veterans. The post was subsequently renamed for Admiral Theodorus Bailey. Shortly after its formation, the post was presented with an oil portrait of the Admiral for display in their meeting rooms.</p>

<p>In 1893, a January fire destroyed all of the buildings on East Main Street from River Street to John St. Unfortunately, in that fire, the lodge rooms and artifacts of the GAR post were lost. </p>

<p>The commander of the post at the time, William H. Finn, had served under Admiral Bailey during the Civil War as one of his staff officers. He became well acquainted with the Admiral and held him in the highest regard.</p>

<p>Following the loss of the portrait in that devastating fire, Finn reached out to Admiral Bailey's son and had a replacement portrait sent to Chateaugay. This work was done by the original artist, a man named U. D. Tenney.</p>

<p>As mentioned above, thanks to the generosity of the local American Legion Post, the Historical Society was able to request the loan of the admiral's portrait for our exhibit. His placement in the display proved to be a highlight of the summer program. This impressive work drew much attention and praise from exhibit visitors.</p>

<p>Post #875 has generously decided to allow the Admiral's portrait to remain in the Historical Society’s Archival Center for the foreseeable future. </p>

<p>This impressive work will greatly add to our presentation of Chateaugay history. The extended loan of this portrait is much appreciated by the Society, and we look forward to many more visits by the public as they enjoy this prominent portrait on display in our Archival Center. </p>

<p>This entire tale of Admiral Bailey, his family and their presence in Chateaugay, William Finn and his role in our town’s past and his service under the Admiral, and the establishment and role of the Grand Army of the Republic post in Chateaugay’s history will all be recounted in an upcoming issue of the Historical Society’s quarterly newsletter.</p>

<p>This wonderful portrait is just part of a story woven of many threads which all come together to tell another interesting and engaging account of Chateaugay’s past.</p>

<p>We are honored to be hosting the Admiral. He will be well cared for and much appreciated during his time in the Archival Center.</p>

<p>Watch for the publication of the upcoming article that will pull together all these separate, but related, parts of this portrait story in a future newsletter issue.</p>

<p>Thank you again to Commander Gene Nesbitt and the entire American Legion Post #875, for their generosity with this long-term loan and their role in helping us to present and display artifacts and objects from Chateaugay’s past.</p>
